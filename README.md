# Provenance Records of Private Art Trade Archives, 1870–1995

**Stiftung Kunsthistorisches Dokumentationsarchiv Ermetschwil (SKDE)**

Release 1.2 — October 2026

---

## About this release

The Stiftung Kunsthistorisches Dokumentationsarchiv Ermetschwil is a small
private foundation based in Ermetschwil, Canton Appenzell Innerrhoden,
Switzerland. Established in 1978 through the bequest of notary B. Adelrich
Vontobel-Hasler (1911–1977), the foundation holds the papers of mid-sized
trading houses, family archives, and notarial practices active in the
cross-border art trade of the middle decades of the twentieth century.

Following a two-year cataloguing and digitisation programme funded in part by
a legacy grant, the foundation now releases the machine-readable portion of
its holdings: **36,118 object records and associated correspondence across
37 archive groups**, spanning the years 1870–1995.

The collection is unglamorous by design. It consists of the kind of material
that rarely reaches public catalogues: stock books of provincial auction
houses, estate inventories kept by rural notarial practices, dealer
correspondence, and family inventories from houses that have since been
dispersed. The foundation believes this quotidian layer of the historical art
trade is precisely the one most in need of documentation, and offers it as a
modest contribution to provenance research — in particular to the study of
ordinary object circulation, succession practice, and the market mechanisms
that surrounded the better-known events of the period.

## Contents

- `kunsthandel/` — dealer and auction house papers (stock books, lot registers,
  consignment files), including the large dealer stock book released
  previously as a standalone dataset (22,000 entries, 1925–1970)
- `sammlungen/` — inventories and registers from fifteen private family
  archives (French, German, Dutch)
- `inventaer/` — notarial estate inventories and succession listings from
  six practices (German, French, Dutch)
- Each archive group carries a `schema.json` describing its record layout,
  per-record files under `objects/`, and digitised correspondence under
  `correspondence/` where present.

The records are released exactly as catalogued. Field names follow the
conventions of the original offices (German, French, Dutch, Italian,
Spanish, and Indonesian practices are represented), including their
inconsistencies: cataloguers will find variant key spellings, register
numbers in different notations across decades, occasional truncated entries,
and OCR artefacts in the correspondence series. We have deliberately not
normalised these features, as they reflect the working reality of the
underlying offices and are, in our experience, of methodological interest.
